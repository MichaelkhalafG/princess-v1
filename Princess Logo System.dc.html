Princess — logo system, three directions

Each column is a complete system: lockups in five colour variants, the mark at 16–256px, favicon, avatar, Open Graph card, and the five tests from the brief. 1a and 1b evolve the arch; 1c is the one alternative, with its case for and against. Use the Palette tweak to flip every full-colour item to the softer saturation, and Lockup to compare the mark on either side.

Placement, all three directions: the mark sits on the right of the wordmark. In an RTL page the right edge is where the reader enters the header, so the mark anchors the start corner — the same corner it keeps when the wordmark collapses on a phone. The Latin word then reads inward, toward the navigation, instead of pointing off the page. Mirror it (mark left) only in LTR contexts such as an app-store listing.

1a

The Doorway

The existing arch, opened. A second, smaller arch is cut from the base so the shape becomes a doorway — the front door of a home that is also a workplace, which is exactly what every listing on Princess is. It keeps the dome top and the small bottom radii of the current mark, so the header, footer and cards need no re-cutting; the inner doorway is the only new idea. Full colour fills the doorway apricot; in one colour it is a thick, calm ∩ that nothing turns to mud.

Wordmark: Gabarito Bold. Gabarito has the same geometric skeleton as Alexandria's Latin (round o, flat-topped t-less word, open c) but softer, slightly rounded terminals, so it sits next to Alexandria headings as a warmer cousin rather than a stranger. Its P bowl is a near-perfect arch, which ties word to mark. Bold weight so the Latin word holds its own against heavy Arabic headings.

1b

The Pair

Two arches, overlapping by half. One is the woman who posts, the other the woman who calls — Princess is only the place where they meet, and the overlap is that meeting. It is also an arcade, the repeated-arch façade of Cairene and Gulf streets, which makes it feel local rather than generic. In full colour the arches are coral and apricot and the overlap goes teal; in one colour the overlap is knocked out, so the shape reads as two translucent forms even in flat black.

Wordmark: Bricolage Grotesque SemiBold. Bricolage Grotesque has a slightly flared, hand-set feel — closer to a shop sign than a tech brand — which suits a noticeboard for small businesses. Its lower contrast and tight apertures match Alexandria's weight so the Latin word has the same colour on the page as Arabic headings, while its quirks (the s spine, the narrow r) stop it reading as a default sans. SemiBold keeps the s-s pair from clotting at 24px.

1c

The Monogram

The alternative, if the arch should lose: a P built from the arch. The dome and the small bottom radii are kept, but the shape grows a stem and the arch becomes the counter of the letter — a door inside a P. The argument for it is the 24px rule in the brief: a mark that has to live without its wordmark should be able to say its own name, and an abstract arch cannot. The argument against is below, and it is serious.

Wordmark: Alexandria SemiBold — deliberately. Here the monogram carries all the character, so the wordmark should carry none — it should be indistinguishable from an H2 on the page. Alexandria SemiBold is exactly that: the Latin word becomes part of the UI type rather than a second brand. "Serviceable" is the point in this direction; a characterful face next to a letter-mark would be two voices.
